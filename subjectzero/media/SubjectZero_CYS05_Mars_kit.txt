THE NUMBERS AND WHERE THEY COME FROM (for replies)
• Pressure: about 0.6 kPa on average (ESA 6.35 mbar), under 1 % of Earth's 101 kPa (Britannica). 95.3 % CO2, 0.13 % O2 (ESA).
• Spit boils: below about 6.3 kPa (the Armstrong limit) water boils at 37 °C. Mars is about ten times below it; even Hellas, the deepest basin (1.16 kPa), is below it.
• ~15 s: time to lose consciousness without pressure (Scientific American / Buckey: "less than 15 seconds"; Sky at Night: "about 15").
• ~90 s: dogs exposed to near-vacuum for up to 90 s always recovered (Brooks AFB, 1965); "1–2 minutes" if returned to pressure. An estimate from animal data.
• The test subject: Guinness record, 14 Dec 1966 (SciAm dates it 1965), Houston: his suit lost pressure in a vacuum chamber, he felt his saliva boil, the chamber was repressurised in 87 s, he recovered.
• Signal delay Earth–Mars: 0 to 22 min one way (NASA NTRS, McBrayer et al.).
• Blue sunset: Curiosity, sol 956 (15 Apr 2015), Gale Crater: fine dust lets blue light through near the sun (NASA/JPL PIA19400).

Reply to keep ready for "wouldn't you freeze first?": No — oxygen runs out long before the cold matters; thin air carries heat away slowly. (Not claimed in the video.)
