THE NUMBERS AND WHERE THEY COME FROM (for replies)
• 8,848.86 m, announced by Nepal and China on 8 Dec 2020.
• Summit air pressure measured at 253 Torr vs 760 at sea level (West et al., J Appl Physiol 1983): each breath carries about a third of the oxygen.
• Climbers spend about 3–4 weeks of acclimatisation rotations before a summit push (Alan Arnette, citing the Himalayan Database).
• January summit average about −36 °C (−33 °F); sustained winds can top 100 mph / 160 km/h (Britannica).
• Near the summit, typical spring wind chill is about −50 °C and bare facial skin freezes in about 5 min, about 1 min in storms (Moore & Semple, High Alt Med Biol 2011): hence "under five minutes".
• 1–3 minutes: FAA time of useful consciousness without oxygen is 2.5–3 min at 28,000 ft and 1–2 min at 30,000 ft; the summit is 29,032 ft. It applies to sudden exposure without acclimatisation (a spawn), it varies by person, and it is the time before judgement fails, not a time of death. Messner and Habeler stood there without bottled oxygen in 1978, after weeks of adapting.
• The summit is Ordovician marine limestone (Qomolangma Formation), about 450–470 million years old, with fossil fragments of trilobites, brachiopods, ostracods and crinoids (IUGS Geoheritage; Montana State University).
