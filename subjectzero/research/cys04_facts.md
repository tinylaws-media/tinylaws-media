# Could you survive? #04 — A Roman winter camp: fact sheet (checked 30 Oct 2026)

Each number used in the episode, with its source. "Spoken" is what the narration says; "screen" what the HUD and the
captions show. The place: **Vindolanda**, a Roman frontier fort in northern England (Northumberland), just south of
where Hadrian's Wall was later built, around **AD 100**, in winter. Main assumption of the episode: she **spawns** in
the fort and is **enrolled as a recruit** (a fiction: women could not enlist in the Roman army), so the question "how long would you last?" becomes "how long would you have
to last": the minimum term of service of the soldiers who garrisoned forts like this one (auxiliary cohorts: Tungrians,
then Batavians). The episode never says she would die; the cold is answered by what the soldiers' own letters show
(parcels of socks and underpants, beer, a bathhouse).

Not used, or left out on purpose:
- **How many soldiers survived their 25 years.** Estimates exist (Scheidel's demographic work; a popular book says
  about 40 % died before the end) but I could not open a primary page with the figure, so no survival odds are given.
- Winter temperatures at Vindolanda around AD 100: no reliable figure found; the episode shows snow and cold but
  says no number.
- "Brittunculi" (the soldiers' sneer for the locals, *Tab. Vindol.* 164): funny, but it mocks a people; left out.
- Real people (Masclus, Cerialis, Claudia Severa, Sulpicia Lepidina) are mentioned or quoted, never drawn. The
  soldiers are never drawn either: the fort is empty but for her and the duck.

Review (30 Oct 2026, independent agent): "fix then publish". Applied: the HUD says "YOU'RE IN FOR ?? YEARS" (not
"YOU'D LAST": 25 years is the term, not a survival time) and the voice adds "If you make it"; "about twenty kilos"
(not "at least"); "just over six hours"; "a Roman manual"; the mid-video letters no longer say "found here" so the
twist (they are real, dug out of the mud here) is not spoiled; "we know all this" removed (the march comes from
Vegetius, the latrines from Housesteads); "oldest known"; the lab now reads the fort's real sick list (Tab. 154).
